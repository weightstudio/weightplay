Animal Bubble Safari - How to Play

Animal Bubble Safari is a free bilingual Kids aiming puzzle with 30 saved levels and six Safari Checks. Players launch animal bubbles, connect groups of three, bank shots off side walls, and rescue lion cubs, giraffes, zebras, elephants, and hippos. The campaign begins with direct shots and precise wall bounces, then adds rescue priorities, stone barriers, leaf bubbles, two-hit honey, row-clearing clouds, sideways wind, moving rows, and four power bubbles. Level 30 combines all four blockers, three rescue animals, wind, shifting rows, and Rainbow, Line Clear, Burst, and Swap shots. There is no countdown, account, purchase, advertising request, or public ranking.

Safari Rescue Story

A chain of watering holes connects the grassland, canyon, riverbank, leafy forest, cloud bridge, and monsoon plain. Curious young animals followed floating bubbles into the route and became surrounded by colorful clusters. The player travels with the lion guide and a bubble launcher, opening safe paths one habitat at a time. A completed level means its matching goal or rescue group is cleared before the launcher runs out of prepared bubbles.

The barriers belong to the route rather than an enemy army. Stone blocks redirect a shot. Leaf bubbles drop when a matching group clears beside them. Sticky honey needs two direct impacts. A cloud releases its entire row when struck. Every fifth level is a Safari Check that recombines the current region’s skills. Completing the Grand Safari Reunion at Level 30 frees the final three animals and finishes the authored journey.

How the Bubble System Works

  • The launcher shows the current and next bubble, so a player can plan two colors ahead.
  • Drag or hold to aim and release to fire. Arrow keys adjust the same logical aim; Enter or Space shoots.
  • A shot follows the visible line, reflects from side walls, and attaches beside the bubble it actually touches. It does not jump to a hidden distant grid cell.
  • Three or more connected bubbles of one animal color clear. Bank levels count a match only after the projectile has touched a wall.
  • Rescue levels place one to three animals inside bubbles. Those exact bubbles must clear before the shot supply ends.
  • Rainbow adopts the struck color; Line Clear removes a row; Burst clears a nearby area and blockers; Swap exchanges colors with the struck bubble.
  • Leaf barriers respond to adjacent matches, honey records two impacts, and clouds clear their row. Wind changes the projectile during flight, while shifting levels move alternating rows after each shot.
  • Stars depend on shots remaining. Best stars, score, unlocks, sound choice, and rescue album stay only in this browser.

How to Play

  1. Press Start Game and swipe the horizontal Stage rail to an unlocked level.
  2. Read the goal, shots, current bubble, next bubble, and short rule hint.
  3. Drag toward a matching group; use the wall when a direct route is blocked.
  4. Release to shoot, then watch the exact collision and new attachment before planning again.
  5. Complete the required matches or rescues before the prepared bubbles run out.

Strategy Tips

  • Use the next-bubble preview before opening a narrow route.
  • A bank shot should hit the wall early enough to approach the cluster from the side.
  • Clear a leaf with a nearby match instead of spending a direct shot on it.
  • Count both honey impacts and avoid leaving its second hit until the last bubble.
  • Clouds can remove useful bubbles too, so check the whole row before striking one.
  • In wind levels, aim slightly against the displayed drift and allow the flight path to bend.
  • Save Burst for dense barriers and Line Clear for a row containing several obstacles or rescue bubbles.
  • When rows shift, re-read the board after every shot instead of repeating the same angle.

Thirty Levels and Six Regions

Levels 1–5: Aim School. Direct matching, one required bank shot, the first zebra rescue, mixed colors, stones, and the first Safari Check establish precise collision.

Levels 6–10: Rescue Routes. Giraffe and elephant rescues, honey-like mazes, double banks, two-animal goals, and the first Rainbow and Line Clear shots expand planning.

Levels 11–15: Barrier Country. Burst and Swap are introduced before leaf, two-hit honey, and row-clearing clouds become real board rules. Cloudbridge Check rescues two animals.

Levels 16–20: Monsoon Paths. East and west wind bend shots, leaves drift, two animals share a windy route, and alternating rows move during the Monsoon Check.

Levels 21–25: Power Trails. Each power bubble is paired with a barrier that makes its area, row, color, or exchange behavior useful. Level 25 includes all four powers and three rescues.

Levels 26–30: Grand Reunion. Leaf-and-stone lanes, honey headwind, moving cloud rows, three-animal rescue, and the final mixed Safari Check demand different aiming decisions rather than larger numbers alone.

Development and Design Notes

The central promise is that a visible collision produces a believable attachment. The game uses continuous projectile movement and checks contact in small steps, then chooses a nearby open position around the bubble actually struck. This avoids the distant snapping that makes aiming feel dishonest. Difficulty grows through approach angles, barrier reactions, rescue placement, wind, row movement, next-bubble planning, and power timing instead of faster animation or tiny targets.

One fixed logical Canvas scales uniformly across phones, tablets, desktop, and short landscape. Touch, mouse, and keyboard feed the same aiming state, while lifecycle cancellation prevents a stale finger release after changing apps from firing a shot. Unlike Bubble Bakery’s tap-to-clear orders, Safari requires trajectory control and wall reflection. Unlike Animal Helper Quest, its decisions are spatial rather than choosing an item from a scene. Kids screens contain no advertising code or reserve.

Parent Note

Animal Bubble Safari may support visual prediction, planning, color grouping, focus, and hand-eye coordination. Adults can ask where a reflected shot will travel, why a cloud row matters, or whether the current bubble should open a route for the next one. Stars and Skill Report are local play feedback, not a school grade, intelligence score, diagnosis, or comparison with another child. Progress stays in the current browser and can disappear if site data is cleared. No child profile is required, and the Kids route requests no advertising.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many levels are included?
There are 30 saved levels and Safari Checks at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30.
Why did my bubble attach beside the target?
It occupies the nearest open position around the bubble physically touched; it should not jump to a distant hidden cell.
How do bank-shot goals work?
The matching projectile must touch a side wall before the group clears.
What do leaf, honey, and cloud barriers do?
Leaves drop beside matches, honey needs two hits, and clouds clear their row.
What are the four power bubbles?
Rainbow changes color, Line Clear removes a row, Burst clears an area, and Swap exchanges colors.
Is there a timer?
No. The limit is the visible supply of prepared bubbles.
Is progress saved?
Unlocks, best stars, scores, album rescues, and sound choice stay only in this browser.
Can I play with touch or keyboard?
Yes. Touch drag, mouse, arrow keys, Enter, and Space share the same aim and collision rules.
Does the Kids page show ads?
No. Animal Bubble Safari creates no advertising request or reserve.
