    Prism Breakers

    Move the paddle beneath each returning light orb, control the rebound with the contact point, and shatter every breakable crystal before all three orbs are lost.

    World and Mission

    A fox keeper restores 30 numbered crystal layouts across six five-stage chapters. The layouts are generated deterministically from the stage number rather than drawn as 30 unique hand-built boards. The chapter names preview the changing rule: Sunlit Quarry, Split Spectrum, Moving Gallery, Mirror Vault, Gravity Forge, and Eclipse Prism.

    How the Systems Work

    Drag anywhere across the arena to align the paddle with a finger or pointer, or use Left and Right or A and D. A centre hit produces a controlled return; an edge hit creates a sharper angle for protected side lanes. Missing an orb spends one of three available orbs.

    Later chapters split orbs through combos, move crystal bands, add permanent mirrors, advance crystal walls, alter the return with gravity, and place fatal void mines. Permanent mirrors are routing tools, not targets.

    How to Play

    1. Choose an unlocked formation and launch the first light orb.
    2. Move early toward the predicted return point and use the paddle centre for recovery.
    3. Use the paddle edge when a narrow angle is needed to reach a protected lane.
    4. After a split, track every active orb and clear all breakable crystals before the last orb falls.

    Strategy Tips

    • Read the downward path instead of chasing the orb after it reaches the paddle.
    • After a difficult recovery, make one safe centre return before creating a sharper angle.
    • In moving formations, aim for the opening the crystal band will reach.
    • Treat void mines as absolute hazards even when a nearby crystal looks easy.

    Progression and Difficulty

    Each chapter groups five stages and adds its own crystal patterns and rules. The paddle controls stay the same while the stage layout and hazards change.

    Developer Design Note

    The visible paddle, collision point, orb trajectory and logical arena use one scaled coordinate system, so touch and mouse contacts create the same return angle. The Result, Retry, Stage Map and Next controls keep the short learning loop intact.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What controls the return angle?
    The point where the orb touches the paddle.
    Do mirrors need to be broken?
    No. Permanent mirrors redirect the orb toward protected crystals.
    Why did the stage fail with an orb still visible?
    A chapter hazard such as a void mine or advancing danger line can also end the attempt.
    How many orbs do I have?
    Each attempt begins with three, and split effects may create additional active orbs.
    Are all 30 crystal layouts hand-built?
    No. The stage number selects a repeatable layout and its chapter rules; the game does not choose a random board each time.
