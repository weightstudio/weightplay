Snack Blocks - How to Play

Snack Blocks is a 30-stage animal snack puzzle about preparing a traveling feast. Every legal swap must create a match, every stage gives a complete move budget, and six chapters change which snacks appear and what counts as success. Progress and personal best scores stay in this browser, so a player can return to the latest unlocked stage without an account.

Story and Snack World

The animals of Snack World are carrying one shared picnic table from the berry fields to the Crown Feast. The route begins on Picnic Path, then crosses a cookie bakery, a grape garden, a cheese workshop, and a pretzel bridge. Each place contributes a different part of the final meal. The board represents the packing table: matching three or more identical snacks clears a prepared batch and lets new food drop into the open spaces.

The six checkpoint stages are the moments when the caravan must prove that the table is ready to travel. Picnic Bell, Bakery Gate, Garden Drum, Workshop Clock, Bridge Banquet, and Crown Table each combine a named snack order with a score requirement. Clearing Stage 30 means every part of the feast has reached the crown table; missing a goal simply returns the same stage for another plan.

Complete Game Flow

  1. Choose Start Game, then drag the horizontal Stage rail to an unlocked card.
  2. Read the card's goal, snack chapter, move budget, and saved best score.
  3. Tap one snack and an adjacent snack, or drag toward a neighbor. The swap is accepted only when it forms a match of three or more.
  4. Matched snacks score points, disappear together, and make the snacks above them fall. New snacks enter from the top.
  5. Automatic follow-up matches form a cascade and multiply the value of that resolution chain.
  6. Use the full move budget. The stage checks its goal only after the final move, then records the score and unlocks the next stage when successful.

Goals, Scoring, and Board Rules

Score stages ask for a total number of points. Collection stages count only the named snack. Pair stages accept either of two named snacks, which creates more possible routes but also asks the player to watch two colors at once. Big-match stages require one clear containing four, five, or six tiles. Cascade stages require a falling refill to create a second or third automatic match. Checkpoint stages combine a specific collection order with a score target, so neither half can be ignored.

A basic clear scores 12 points per tile, multiplied by the current cascade depth. A swap that does not create a match rolls back and does not spend a move. Different stages use different snack pools: some remove two snack types to support deliberate chains, while mastery stages use all six. The current score, moves, and goal progress are always visible above the 7-by-10 board.

Thirty Stages in Six Chapters

  • Stages 1-5, Picnic Path: learn score, strawberry collection, and a four-tile clear before Picnic Bell combines collection and score.
  • Stages 6-10, Cookie Crossing: rotating snack pools introduce two-step cascades, five-tile clears, and cookie-plus-candy collection.
  • Stages 11-15, Grape Garden: grape-focused boards alternate between reduced and expanded pools, ending with Garden Drum's dual order.
  • Stages 16-20, Cheese Workshop: large clears, cheese collection, and the first three-step cascade demand space below planned matches.
  • Stages 21-25, Pretzel Bridge: all-six boards appear more often, pair goals rise, and Bridge Banquet requires both pretzels and a strong score.
  • Stages 26-30, Crown Feast: three-step cascades, six-tile clears, paired candy-and-cheese orders, and the final Crown Table checkpoint combine the full rule set.

Planning Tips

  • Scan the lower half first. A low clear moves more tiles and creates more chances for a cascade.
  • On collection stages, do not take every available match immediately; first check whether it helps move the requested snack into position.
  • For a big-match goal, build around four or five aligned snacks instead of relying on a random refill.
  • For cascade goals, leave a nearly complete match above the row you plan to clear.
  • Checkpoint goals need balance. Watch both counters and change priorities when either collection or score falls behind.

Development and Design

The board is taller than a conventional square match puzzle because the phone layout benefits from large, recognizable animal-snack art while still leaving room for vertical chain planning. Invalid swaps return immediately, which keeps the rule readable without spending a move on an accidental gesture. Matches disappear as one group before refill movement begins, so the player can understand exactly which plan succeeded.

The 30-stage structure was designed around changing information rather than a simple target increase. Snack pools rotate, objective families change, and every fifth stage combines two responsibilities. Touch, pointer drag, and keyboard activation all use the same native tile buttons. There is no timer and no advertising request in this Kids build, allowing players to inspect the board at their own pace.

Parent Note

Snack Blocks is intended for ages 9+ and family play because later stages combine two goals, changing snack pools, and cascade planning. The local Skill Report summarizes only events from the current run. It is supportive play feedback, not an intelligence test, diagnosis, school assessment, or comparison with other children. The game is free, needs no login, and stores unlocked stages and records only in the current browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many stages are included?
There are 30 saved stages in six chapters, with checkpoint stages at 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30.
Why does an invalid swap move back?
A legal swap must immediately make a match of three or more. Invalid swaps return and do not spend a move.
What is a cascade?
It is an automatic match created after cleared snacks fall and the board refills. Consecutive cascades increase the score multiplier.
How do pair goals work?
Tiles of either named snack count toward one combined collection target.
What is different about a checkpoint?
A checkpoint requires both a named snack quantity and a score target before it unlocks the next chapter.
Is progress saved?
Unlocked stages and personal best records are stored locally in this browser. Clearing site data removes them.
Does Snack Blocks work on phones and computers?
Yes. Players can tap or drag on touch screens and use the focusable tile buttons with a keyboard.
Is the game free and account-free?
Yes. It plays in the browser without login and the Kids version makes no advertising request.
Is the Skill Report a formal ability test?
No. It is a friendly summary of this run's score and goal result only.
