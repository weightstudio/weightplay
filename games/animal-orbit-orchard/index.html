How to play

  1. 1. Tune the ringMove the dial until the seed points toward the glowing beacon.
  2. 2. Release on targetTap Release when the orbit marker reaches the beacon angle.
  3. 3. Try again calmlyA miss is safe to retry. There is no timer or life loss.

Results and saves

Three short orbits complete one calm session, and your best release count stays in this browser. The release count includes safe attempts, so it is a useful record of careful tuning rather than a speed score. A completed result names the three beacons and keeps the next action clear.

Practical tips

Use the target angle as a cue, adjust a miss in small steps, and keep the dial within sixteen degrees of the beacon. Read the target before moving the control, watch the seed and beacon relationship, and make one small correction at a time. If a release misses, keep the same orbit and tune again; nothing is lost and there is no timer to rush the decision.

Read each orbit

Treat every orbit as a fresh visual comparison. First find the glowing beacon and read its angle. Next compare that angle with the seed marker on the dial, remembering that the ring wraps from 359 degrees back to 0. The shortest turn is usually the clearest correction, but there is no need to be exact on the first attempt because the target window is forgiving. Before pressing Release, check the marker, the target label, and the round name together. These three cues describe the same decision in different ways. After a miss, use the distance message to decide whether the seed is ahead or behind, then adjust only the amount that the feedback suggests. This keeps the puzzle readable and gives a learner a calm way to improve without hiding the rule.

Finish the session

The session has three authored orbits, each with its own beacon angle and name. Starting a session always opens Orbit 1 with zero releases and the ready prompt. A correct release advances to the next orbit after a short confirmation, while an incorrect release leaves the dial, target, and actions available for another try. When Orbit 3 is complete, the Result surface reports the total and offers Stages, Next Stage, or Replay. Stages returns to the orbit choices, Next Stage stays unavailable because this internal slice has no fourth orbit, and Replay starts the three-orbit sequence from the beginning. Back and Settings remain shared shell actions so navigation, language, and sound stay predictable during a calm repeat.
