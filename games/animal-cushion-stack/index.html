1. Understand the 30-stage trail

Each stage is a layered construction puzzle. The tower area shows the support that is already in place, while the tray offers three possible cushions for the next layer. Your goal is not to build as quickly as possible. Instead, read the support width, compare the choices, and place a cushion that can sit safely inside the support below it. A correct cushion becomes part of the tower and reveals the next decision. Complete every placement in a stage to clear it. The full trail contains 30 stages arranged in six arcs, with a checkpoint every fifth stage.

The opening stages introduce Meadow, Pond, and Moon towers. Stages 4–30 continue the trail with new support widths and cushion combinations, so check the current evidence rather than memorize one answer for the whole chapter.

2. Read support and cushion widths

The support width appears in the Battle hint. Every choice also shows its own width. A safe cushion is narrower than the support and leaves a sensible margin on both sides. A cushion that is wider than the support would overhang, while the smallest option may be too narrow to create the intended stable layer. Compare the numbers first, then use the cushion name, color, and visible size as a second check.

The tower illustration updates only after a successful check. This keeps the settled layers distinct from the choice you are considering. If the support changes from one layer to the next, read the new value again before choosing; the block that worked on the floor is not automatically right for the middle or top.

3. Choose, check, and recover

Select a cushion in the tray, then press Check stability. The selected state and status text confirm which cushion is active before the check. A steady choice plays positive feedback and adds the cushion to the tower. A wobbly choice gives calm error feedback, clears the selection, and leaves the current layer unchanged. You lose no life, time, or completed layer, so use the response as information and try another option.

Clear choice removes a selection before checking if you change your mind. The lookout map remains available during play, and returning to it does not invent a win. When every placement in a stage is steady, that stage clears and the next stage becomes available. Continue through all 30 stages; clearing Stage 30 presents the final chapter Result with your total picks.

4. Improve your fewest-picks result

Your total picks count every checked choice, including wobbly attempts. The best result therefore comes from making each comparison before pressing Check. Start with the support width, rule out the obvious overhang, and then compare the remaining cushions. The best local score records the fewest picks used to complete the full 30-stage chapter; there is no speed bonus and no reason to rush.

On a replay, look for the decision that caused an extra pick rather than racing through every action. The opening Meadow, Pond, and Moon stages teach the basic margin, centered shapes, and a decorative top; later stages combine these reads across longer towers and checkpoints. This progression rewards careful reading and memory without turning a wrong answer into punishment.

5. Controls, sound, and language

All primary actions are ordinary buttons that work with touch, mouse, and keyboard. Keyboard players can move focus with Tab and activate the focused control with Enter or Space. The permanent arrow returns from Battle to the lookout map, while the Main return leads back to the General WeightPlay lobby. The Settings button on Main contains one language selector and one combined Sound switch.

The language choice supports all thirteen WeightPlay locales, including Arabic right-to-left layout. Sound is optional and mirrors the shared WeightPlay audio preference. Visual selected states, status text, widths, and tower changes continue to communicate the result when sound is muted. If browser storage is unavailable, the game remains playable; only the local best may last for the current visit.

6. Strategy and fair-play notes

A reliable strategy is: read the current support, compare every visible width, choose the cushion with a safe centered margin, and check only after the selected label matches your intention. Do not assume that the largest remaining cushion is always correct. Some layers use a snug rectangular cushion, while others use cloud, leaf, moon, or flag shapes with different safe widths.

Cushion Stack has no timer, life loss, ranking, purchases, ads inside the game canvas, or account requirement. Every puzzle is deterministic, so the same lookout presents the same fair evidence on a replay. The game does not require guessing hidden physics; the displayed support and choice widths are the decision tools. Stop after any lookout or replay the full journey when you want to improve the fewest-picks result.

FAQ

Is progress saved?
Yes. The fewest-picks best is saved only in this browser when storage is available.
What happens after a wobbly choice?
The current layer stays safe, the selection clears, and you may immediately choose again.
Do I need to finish quickly?
No. There is no timer; careful comparison is the intended way to play.
