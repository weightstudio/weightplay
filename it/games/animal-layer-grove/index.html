Puzzle di Sovrapposizione

Compare the target habitat window, then rebuild thirty authored scenes across six arcs with changing layer sets, decoys, wind reading, and echo layers.

How to play

The central board is your current construction, not the answer. Each numbered row and lettered column names a square. Select a layer thumbnail to outline its entire footprint, including currently hidden squares. The top row of the layer tray is closest to you. Use the up and down arrows to exchange neighboring layers. A solid square hides every layer behind it; a transparent square reveals the first solid square underneath. The miniature silhouettes always show the current orientation. On later stages, a circular-arrow control rotates only its marked layer clockwise by a quarter turn. Selection and inspection are free. Undo restores the previous arrangement without returning an already spent move.

Read the clues

A coordinate clue such as B2 → ◆ constrains just one visible square; it does not tell you the complete arrangement. A whole-scene clue counts all visible squares with its symbol. Row and column clues count only their named line. Hidden squares do not contribute to any count. All clues must hold together. Checking evaluates the scene you built rather than comparing your list against a stored answer order. A correct count can still coexist with a wrong coordinate, so inspect the entire set. After an unsuccessful check, the unmet clues and relevant cells are highlighted. Change the scene to clear that feedback. Checking never removes lives or deducts moves.

Thirty new puzzles

The first five puzzles introduce occlusion with three layers. Stages 6–10 add a fourth layer and visible counts. Stages 11–15 permit one marked layer to rotate; 16–20 require thinking about two independent rotations and line counts. Stages 21–25 use five layers, and 26–30 combine five layers with two rotatable pieces. These are thirty fixed, reproducible puzzles, not a newly randomized answer after each reset. Later puzzles have more possible constructions and overlapping constraints. Completing a puzzle unlocks the next one; already completed puzzles remain replayable. There is no compulsory timer or move limit. Take as long as needed to form and test a hypothesis.

Saves and replay

This rebuilt campaign uses a separate local save. The older sorting campaign is not deleted and is not silently treated as thirty solved new puzzles. Completion, best move counts and earned stars are stored on this browser when storage is available. Private browsing, storage restrictions or clearing site data can prevent persistence; the game remains playable for the current session. There is no account or cloud synchronization in this game. A clear earns one star; clearing without a hint earns two; an unassisted clear within the displayed post-result efficient-move threshold earns three. Reset and Undo do not erase spent moves or hint use, so a record cannot be improved merely by hiding previous attempts. Replay begins a fresh attempt.

Reasoning tips

Begin with a clue shared by two competing layers and ask which must be closer to the viewer. Then check whether that choice breaks a count elsewhere. Inspect silhouettes before rotating: rotating a single layer changes its footprint but never moves the other layers. Try a reversible experiment when uncertain and use Undo to compare. A hint explains the reasoning method; it does not place a layer or reveal a final order. The optional stars are a replay challenge rather than a condition for unlocking the next stage. All controls can be reached with a keyboard as well as a pointer, and geometric symbols distinguish layers without relying only on color. Leaving requires confirmation while an attempt is active.
