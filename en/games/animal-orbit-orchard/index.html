How to play

  1. 1. Tune the ringMove the dial until the seed points toward the glowing beacon. Start by finding the bright beacon and reading the target angle. Then compare the seed marker with that direction on the ring. The dial wraps around, so a small turn across zero can be shorter than a large turn in the other direction. Take a moment to look at the round name and the target label before you release. The goal is a clear visual match, not a fast reaction, and the forgiving target window gives you room to learn the relationship between the marker and the orchard light.
  2. 2. Release on targetTap Release when the orbit marker reaches the beacon angle. Check the seed, the target label, and the round counter as one small checklist before committing. If the marker is close but not aligned, adjust the dial by a few degrees and look again. A successful release shows calm confirmation and moves the session forward. The action does not depend on a timer, a life count, or a hidden combo, so a player can focus on the shape of the dial and the position of the beacon. Every round uses a named authored angle, which makes the feedback easy to compare with the next decision.
  3. 3. Try again calmlyA miss is safe to retry: there is no timer or life loss. Reset returns your aim to zero without leaving the stage. Some stages move the beacon after a miss or change which lane is open; read the new cue before trying again.

Results and saves

Clears, the next unlocked stage, and your best release count for each stage stay in this browser. Older best-release data is preserved. Cleared stages remain open to replay; locked stages cannot enter Battle.

Practical tips

Read the light and name before you adjust. A wrong release is safe. Drift changes the target only where the stage says so; the Prism Clamp can hold one drifting beacon, but never opens a shaded lane.

Read each stage

The ring wraps from 359 degrees to zero. Follow the named beacon; when angles are hidden, use its position. In stages with decoys or shade, choose the correct light and an open lane before releasing. A drift rule moves the target only after a miss. The Prism Clamp prevents one such move per stage, not a blocked lane. Review ordered lights with the sequence button.

Complete the campaign

The Moonlit Orchard Routes contain six five-stage arcs: Dewlight Ring, Moon Current, Shadow-Grove Paths, Firefly Signals, Prism Weave, and Night Orchard. Checkpoints appear at Stages 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30. Stage 5 awards the Prism Clamp for one use on each later stage. Ordered lights advance only after a correct release; a miss keeps the current light available for another try. Choose an open lane when shade appears, and remember that the final lane choice can affect the next starting angle. Stage 30 is the four-light Nightkeeper finale. There is no timer, life loss, purchase, or forced wait. Your progress is local to this browser, and every cleared route can be replayed.

Frequently asked questions

Progress is stored locally by stage. Completing Stage 5 unlocks the Prism Clamp; later stages become available in order.
