The race

You drive a compact kart through closed, three-dimensional circuits with block-animal opponents. The goal is not simply to hold the accelerator: choose where to turn in, judge the next corner, and decide when a drift is worth the speed you lose. The visible road, its shoulders and the guardrail form the driving space. Gold rings, marked boost pads, wet road patches and orange cones are real gameplay objects. The floating gold arrow points to your next required checkpoint. A finished lap counts only after those checkpoints have been crossed in order.

Driving and objectives

Auto-throttle is on. A/D or ←/→ steer; S/↓ brakes; hold Space and steer to drift, then release for a boost. Shift/X uses nitro. R returns to the last checkpoint with +3 s. Touch buttons support multiple fingers. P/Escape pauses. Read every objective on the selected card before entering. A race may require a finishing position, elapsed drifting time, collected rings, a time limit or a maximum number of contacts. Multiple requirements must all succeed. Follow an opponent closely on a straight to benefit from slipstream, then leave the draft to pass. Holding a drift builds a short exit boost; releasing too early gives no reward. Nitro is a separate resource. In conservation races it does not refill from driving, rings or drifting, although a marked road pad still supplies a temporary boost.

A changing campaign

Circuits use different layouts, including reverse routes. Long corners reward controlled drifting, wet patches reduce grip, and collecting rings can require a wider line than the fastest route. Elevation changes and flag-marked crosswinds also affect steering. Events combine these conditions with nitro limits or clean-driving objectives. Completing the displayed requirements unlocks the next race; unlocked events remain replayable. Faster times can add stars but never replace the actual objectives.

Racing tips

Brake before a tight corner, not after the guardrail is already in your path. Start a drift while you still have room and release when the exit opens. Save nitro for a straight: extra speed cannot rescue a poor entry angle. Watch your ring count before the final lap ends. Recovery is a safety tool, not a shortcut; it empties nitro and adds three seconds. Contact counts include rivals, cones and the boundary, with a short cooldown to avoid counting one sustained impact every frame.

Design notes

This is an arcade handling model, not a real-world driving simulator. Player and AI cars use the same fixed-step movement rules; opponents steer toward the road rather than teleporting to an expected position. World geometry, elevation, cars, lighting and the follow camera are rendered in real time. The interface stays in shared WeightPlay scenes so changing vehicles does not create another menu framework. Garage choices trade speed, acceleration and grip. Tuning can help a preferred driving style but does not change the checkpoint rules. There is no multiplayer, remote leaderboard, daily event or ghost opponent in this build.

Player and Save Information

Use a browser with WebGL 2 and keyboard, multitouch or a standard gamepad. Gamepad steering uses the left stick or directional pad; the south face button drifts, the east button or right trigger boosts, the left trigger brakes and Start pauses. No account or payment is required by this game. Progress is stored in this browser, not in the cloud. Clearing browser data can remove it. Storage failure keeps the current session usable and displays a warning. Losing focus pauses a running race; returning never silently fast-forwards it.

Questions

Why did I finish but fail?

Finishing the laps is only one condition. Check the Result list for an unmet position, drift, ring, contact or time target.

How do I unlock the other cars?

Earn campaign stars. The hare, panda and penguin require 6, 18 and 36 stars. Selection is free once the threshold is reached.

Can I farm the Result buttons?

No. Each race settles its reward once. Replay creates a new race; it does not pay the previous result a second time.

What happens when 3D rendering fails?

The simulation stops and shows recovery actions. Retry reconstructs that race; returning to Stages keeps your previously saved progress.
