Maze Chase

Maze Chase is an original fixed-maze collection game. Guide Orla through three increasingly fast stages, gather every star mote, read four distinct Wisp chase patterns, and activate Beacons to turn a dangerous corridor into a short counterattack window.

World and Mission

The moonlit archive is built from one readable maze rather than a random field. Each stage keeps the corridor language familiar while moving Wisp homes and increasing pressure, so route knowledge becomes a useful advantage without turning the run into memorization alone.

Four Wisps behave differently: one closes distance directly, one predicts the current heading, one aims beyond the next turn, and one wanders through open choices. Their role markers make the threat readable before the player commits to a corridor.

How the Systems Work

Move Orla one corridor step at a time with Arrow keys or WASD. On touch, use the visible direction pad or swipe the maze; the same route state is used by keyboard and touch input.

Every star mote must be collected to clear a stage. A Beacon is a larger power item: collecting it gives a limited window in which Wisps can be chased and scored instead of avoided.

A collision costs a life and resets the current position while preserving the stage route. The run ends only after the available lives are spent, so a mistake is feedback rather than an instant restart.

Score, Best, Stage, lives, sound, Restart, a leave confirmation, and success or failure Result recovery are part of the same run flow. Best is stored locally in this browser.

How to Play

  1. Start from Main and watch the first corridor opening before choosing a direction.
  2. Collect the small star motes while keeping one turn available as an escape route.
  3. Read the Wisp markers and avoid walking into a corridor that removes every safe option.
  4. Take a Beacon when the chase is close enough to reward a reversal, then use the power window to score a Wisp.
  5. Clear all three stages to finish the route, or use Restart to practice the current opening again.

Strategy Tips

  • Do not commit to the deepest corridor first; leave a turn that lets you change heading when a Wisp approaches.
  • Use the direct Wisp to learn when to rotate early, then watch the predicted and ambush paths before taking a Beacon.
  • A safe detour that collects two nearby motes is often better than a short line toward one isolated mote.
  • Save a Beacon for a crowded junction when several Wisps are visible, because one reversal can create both score and space.
  • When a collision resets the player, use the grace period to re-read the next corridor instead of repeating the same turn.

Campaign and Difficulty Growth

Stage 1 teaches collection and corridor reading with slower Wisp movement.

Stage 2 rotates the Wisp starting homes and raises pursuit speed so route choices must be made earlier.

Stage 3 combines the fastest pressure with the same fixed maze, rewarding Beacon timing, short detours, and calm recovery.

FAQ

How do I clear a stage?
Visit every collectible cell in the fixed maze, including the Beacon cells. The remaining collectible count must reach zero to clear the stage; chasing Wisps for bonus points is optional.
Do the Wisps use the same chase logic?
No. Direct, predictive, ambush, and wandering Wisps choose routes differently and show distinct role markers.
What does a Beacon do?
A Beacon starts a short power window in which nearby Wisps can be chased for bonus points.
What happens after a collision?
You lose one life and return to the current route with a brief grace period. The stage remains active until lives run out.
Can I play on a phone?
Yes. Swipe the maze or use the on-screen direction pad; keyboard arrows and WASD are also supported on desktop.
Is progress saved?
The Best score and sound preference are stored locally in this browser. No account is required.
