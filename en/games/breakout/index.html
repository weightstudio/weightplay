Game overview and mission

Breakout is a thirty-stage lane-planning arcade campaign set across six compact brick routes. You control the paddle rather than an automatic ball: read the highlighted legal brick, move left or right until the paddle shares its column, then choose when to Serve. A correct shot removes one brick and adds score; a shot from the wrong lane still spends a shot and leaves the field unchanged. Success means clearing every brick in the selected stage, reviewing the Result, and unlocking the next authored pattern. The challenge is not speed alone. It is reading the current route, changing lanes deliberately, and finishing with fewer wasted serves.

How to play

  1. Start a round and read the highlighted brick and the current target lane.
  2. Use Left and Right to align the paddle with the target column.
  3. Press Serve to spend one shot; an aligned shot clears its brick and raises the score.
  4. When a column is clear, follow the next-target direction cue before serving again.
  5. Clear all twelve bricks, then use Play again or return to Main to begin another round.

Complete game loop

Each stage begins from the Stage catalog and uses a fixed, learnable brick pattern. In Battle, the highlighted brick and message identify the current legal target. Left and Right move one lane at a time, Serve resolves exactly one attempt, Hint restates the next useful lane, and Restart restores that stage. There are no lives, hidden purchases, or random power-ups. A miss increases the shot count but does not erase progress within the round. Clearing the final brick opens Result, where the stage score, shot total, and best result explain the outcome. Continue to the next unlocked stage, return to the catalog, or replay to reduce wasted shots.

Progression and difficulty

The short route stays focused on aim, timing, and readable consequences. Missed serves still count as shots, while cleared bricks remain cleared, so careful lane changes improve the rematch goal.

Stages 1–5 teach direct alignment and single-row routes. Stages 6–15 add denser columns and longer direction changes, making the cue after a cleared lane more important. Stages 16–25 combine tall stacks with wider travel, so an early unnecessary move can produce several extra shots. Stages 26–30 are mastery patterns that ask you to alternate distant lanes while preserving a clean route. Every fifth stage closes one of the six campaign arcs. Completed stages remain replayable, and the next authored stage unlocks after a clear; difficulty grows through route structure rather than faster controls or hidden rules.

Practical tips

  • Do not auto-serve after clearing a lane; the next cue may ask for a different column.
  • Use the highlighted target as advice, while keeping control of every paddle move and Serve.
  • Check the Result shot count to decide whether to replay for a cleaner clear.

Developer design note

This version turns the familiar brick-breaking idea into a deterministic, turn-sized decision game. Separating Move from Serve makes every miss attributable and keeps touch, mouse, and keyboard play equivalent. The highlighted brick, lane cue, shot counter, Hint, and Result all report the same underlying stage state, so feedback never asks the player to guess what the game accepted. Thirty authored patterns provide progression without changing the six-lane control grammar. The responsive Stage and Battle surfaces preserve the same decisions from phone portrait through desktop, while local saving supports short return sessions without requiring an account, cloud profile, or network connection.

FAQ

What does a highlighted brick mean?
It marks the current legal target for the paddle lane.
What happens after a missed Serve?
The shot count increases without clearing a brick, and the status explains the next route cue.
Is progress saved?
Best results and preferences are saved only in this browser.
How does the 30-stage campaign advance?
Clear every brick in the selected stage to unlock the next authored pattern. Completed stages stay available for replay.
