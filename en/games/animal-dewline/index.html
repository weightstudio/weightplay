FAQ

Is progress saved?
Yes, only in this browser.

Plan a calm sequence

Begin by reading all three values before touching a control. The meadow note is a small plan, not a timed test. Compare the targets, notice which valve is furthest away, and decide on a gentle order. A valve can move up or down one step at a time, so you can watch how each change affects the total. Keeping the whole note in view makes the linked system easier to understand.

Read the live total

The current total is a helpful checkpoint, but it is not the only answer. Two settings can share a total while still missing different targets. Look at each valve card, then use the total to confirm the direction of your adjustment. If the total rises too far, step back with the same small controls. This rhythm keeps the puzzle readable and gives every choice a clear cause and effect.

Use feedback as a clue

When a check does not match, Dewline names the first valve to revisit. Treat that message as a clue rather than a penalty. Return to the named card, make one considered change, and check again. There are no lives to lose, no countdown, and no hidden score multiplier. The calm feedback loop is designed for learning the relationship between the three linked flows.

Finish each meadow

A correct flow wakes the flowers and records the meadow in your local progress. The next meadow introduces a different target pattern while keeping the same understandable controls. You may return to the meadow map whenever you want, and a completed result shows the truthful number of tries. Finish all three meadows to see the complete dawn scene, or replay a meadow to practise a cleaner sequence.

Progress and privacy

Your best tries and completed meadows stay in this browser only. No account, network connection, purchase, timer, or public ranking is needed. If you clear site data or use another browser, the local record will not follow you. That boundary keeps the game lightweight and private while still making a returning session feel continuous. Settings contains the language and sound choices used by the game.

Replay thoughtfully

Replay is an invitation to notice patterns, not a requirement to chase a score. Try a different valve order and observe whether the same total can hide a different mismatch. The map keeps every meadow available after it opens, while the result screen explains what you achieved. If you pause, close the page, or return later, the local progress remains ready for the next quiet session. Choose the pace that feels comfortable, and let each small adjustment teach you something about the meadow's connected water.
