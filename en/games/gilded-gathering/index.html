Gilded Gathering

Arrange tables, keep aisles open and welcome every guest with light, tea and a little quiet.

Next gathering: 1 / 60

Venue planning · Spatial strategy · Resource puzzle

Goal

Help Taro prepare a golden festival hall while Panko hosts the stage. Every party needs its own table, a connected aisle from the entrance and a lantern no more than two grid steps away. Read the needs on the guest cards before placing the first fixture. A pretty arrangement is only ready when people can reach and use it.

How to play

Choose a furniture tool and tap an empty tile to build. Choose Seat guests, select a party card and tap its table. One table cannot belong to two parties. Move changes a fixture’s position in one action; Remove returns its full budget. Undo reverses the complete previous action. When you open the hall, guests follow the actual connected aisles to their assigned seats. A failed check names the missing need and leaves your plan available to edit.

Controls and rules

Music guests want a table within two grid steps of Panko’s stage. Tea guests need a powered tea station within two steps. Quiet guests sit at least three steps from the stage, or directly beside a sound screen. Window guests need a table on the left or right edge. Combined symbols require all their conditions. Furniture blocks walking, so always leave a reachable empty tile beside each table. Lanterns use one power; tea stations use two. A battery, introduced in Chapter 2, adds two power but takes a tile and three budget.

Finishing and feedback

There are 60 saved stages in two chapters. The second chapter shows two guest lists from the beginning. After the first act, fixtures stay in place and you may make at most two build, remove or move actions; seat assignments can change freely. Planning for both lists can save those actions. A clear earns one star; matching the stage’s construction-cost target and total walking-distance target adds one star each. Replays can improve those records without risking an earlier clear. Reset and replay are free.

The idea behind the hall

In November 2026, reporting continued on a White House ballroom construction visit after the US midterm elections. This game turns the venue-planning setting into a fictional gathering: you balance seats, open aisles and guest needs with limited space and power. Its animal hosts, room problems and rules are original fiction.

A note from the developer

The hall is designed around the empty space you leave. A shared lantern is economical, but the tile it occupies can close an aisle; a screen makes a close seat quiet, but may take the only route to tea. In later stages, compare the next guest list before committing a fixture. We chose a fixed camera and tap-to-place controls so the whole planning problem stays visible on phones and desktop. No timer forces you to act before you understand a check.

Player and save information

No sign-in is needed. Clears, best records, the last layout and tutorial progress are stored locally in this browser. Clearing browser data can remove them. Optional hall styles are permanent shared-wallet purchases: Midnight wood costs 5 Diamonds and Glass festival costs 10. They change materials only, never the puzzle rules. The basic hall, every stage, every tool and all retries remain free. If saving fails, the result stays available for a save retry.

Questions about the hall

Why is a lit table still rejected?

Check the assigned party, its other needs, the total power and a reachable empty tile beside the table. Light alone does not provide a walking route.

Can a quiet guest sit near the stage?

Yes. Place a sound screen directly beside that table. The screen occupies floor space, so check the aisle again.

What counts as a second-act change?

Building, removing or moving a fixture uses one of the two actions. Assigning parties to existing tables is free. Undo restores the previous action and its change count.

How does the battery help?

One battery adds two power. It costs three construction budget and occupies one tile, so it can support another lantern while making the layout tighter.

Do hall styles make stages easier?

No. Both optional permanent purchases change the hall’s appearance only. Every stage can be cleared in the free basic style.
