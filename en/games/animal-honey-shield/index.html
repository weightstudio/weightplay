Animal Honey Shield

Draw nectar lines, brace them against terrain, and keep Pip safe while bees try to shove the walls aside.

Protect Pip with the shortest strong line

Pip has wandered beside Cloverlight Meadow's hives. Draw barriers while the startled bees fly, then reinforce openings before they reach the puppy.

How to play

  1. Drag across the meadow to draw a glowing nectar barrier.
  2. The bees wait while you draw. Releasing your first valid line starts the wave; keep drawing repairs during it.
  3. Survive until the timer reaches zero without letting a bee touch Pip.

Rules and results

Lines never break. Bees use any open route first. With no route they push, twist, and flex a loose wall. A loop on one stone is only a pivot: the swarm peels it tight around that stone. Only one stroke hooked around two separated terrain points turns gold and holds.

Start by studying the meadow before you draw. Pip is safest when the barrier creates a short route that bees cannot simply walk around, but a long wall is expensive and gives the swarm more surface to push. Leave a little breathing room around the puppy so a repair does not trap your next stroke. When a barrier flashes red, repair that weak section first instead of replacing the whole shape. The first valid line starts the wave, so you have time to plan while the meadow is quiet. Once bees are moving, watch their approach rather than the line you are drawing: a safe-looking wall can still leave an open corner behind Pip. Two separated terrain anchors are stronger than one loop. A single stone lets a wall pivot or peel; wrapping the same stroke around two distinct stones, corners, or approved terrain points makes the wall hold and glow gold. If bees push a loose wall, do not chase the wall with frantic redraws. Wait for the gap they are trying to open, then add a short hook that closes that route. Nectar is limited, and efficient repairs usually earn more stars than a second large circle. Before each later chapter, reread the chapter hint: new hives, split approaches, gusts, fragile gaps, and mixed waves each change what the shortest safe line should look like. After a clear, the next route unlocks automatically and your best stars remain in this browser. A failed attempt costs no account data; simply return to the stage list, study the red opening, and try a different shape. You can also replay an earlier route to practice efficient shapes, compare the stars earned from short repairs, and learn which side of the meadow gives the bees the fewest choices. The stage summary records your progress, while the guide remains available whenever you need to refresh the basic rules.

Practical tips

A straight bridge and a one-stone circle are both loose. Use two separated wraps, opposite hooks, or a real terrain slot.

FAQ

Can I draw after the bees start?
Yes. Add another line or connect a loose wall to terrain while nectar remains.
Do bees get hurt?
No. Nectar barriers gently bounce the startled bees back toward the flowers.
What should I play next?
Try One Line for careful tracing or Animal Guard Yard for a larger defense challenge.

Meadow report

Draw, brace, and hold

  1. Drag one short barrier between Pip and the hive.
  2. Open route: bees detour to Pip. No route: they move a spanning wall sideways toward the nearest open edge. The instant a gap opens, they release it and attack.
  3. One stone cannot lock a wall. Wrap or hook the same stroke around two separated terrain points; only that two-point lock turns gold.
