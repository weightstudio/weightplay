Yukon Solitaire

Yukon Solitaire deals the full deck onto seven tableau columns. Build descending red-black sequences, move any eligible face-up group, and complete four suit foundations without a Stock.

DifficultyMedium to Hard
Estimated Play Time5-20 minutes
Skills TrainedPlanning · Spatial Reasoning · Focus

World and Mission

Yukon keeps all 52 cards on the table but loosens the group rule: any face-up card can carry every face-up card below it. That freedom creates dramatic reveals, but it also makes early empty columns precious.

The game rewards exposing facedown cards quickly while choosing when to use a flexible group move and when to preserve a King landing space.

How the Systems Work

The tableau uses seven columns with a facedown section and a face-up section. Build descending alternating-color sequences.

Unlike FreeCell or Klondike, any face-up card can start a move with all face-up cards below it, even if that group is not a complete sequence.

Empty columns accept only a King or a group beginning with a King. Foundations build upward by suit from Ace to King.

There is no Stock. Hint highlights one legal move and Undo restores the complete tableau and foundation state.

How to Play

  1. Select an exposed card or any face-up group.
  2. Move it onto the next higher rank of the opposite color, or place a single eligible card on its Foundation.
  3. Expose facedown cards by clearing their covering cards.
  4. Use an empty column only when a King-led group can occupy it.
  5. Complete all four suit foundations to win.

Strategy Tips

  • Expose facedown cards before making surface-only changes.
  • Treat an empty column as a King reserve, not just free space.
  • Use arbitrary face-up groups to reveal hidden cards, but do not strand a useful lower card.
  • Move Aces and low foundation cards when they open a column or unblock a face-down card.
  • Use Undo to compare a bold group move with a slower reveal route.

Campaign and Difficulty Growth

Every deal uses all 52 cards on the tableau and has no Stock phase.

Early rounds teach the difference between moving a sequence and moving any face-up group.

Higher challenge comes from preserving landing space and planning multiple reveals ahead.

FAQ

Is there a Stock?
No. All 52 cards begin on the tableau.
Can any face-up group move?
Yes. Any face-up card may carry every face-up card below it.
What can fill an empty column?
A King or a group whose first card is a King.
Do tableau suits alternate?
Yes. Tableau builds descend by rank in alternating colors.
Can I undo?
Yes. Undo restores the full prior tableau and foundations.
